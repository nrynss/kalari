## Method

The 0.2.3 comparison runs three coding models (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.3 Flash, MiMo V2.6 Flash) across three upgrades (payouts, settlements, disbursements, all `pagination_changed`), five repeats each, two arms. One arm is OpenCode 2.2.5 with microsandbox-mcp and no Kalari tools. The other is OpenCode 2.2.5 with the `kalari serve` MCP entry (five tools), no shell, no microsandbox-mcp, persistent project stores, and the operator observer through the per-run broker. That is 45 sessions per arm. One repeat each way for Qwen3.8 27B and Muse Glimmer 30B sits outside the headline by user decision, bringing the published total to 51 sessions per arm, 102 sessions, served through the evidence API from the published bundle. Each session caps at 900 seconds; two GLM payouts sessions alone stopped at the cap. The repeat rows live with the filmed takes in the published bundle behind `slides/data/results.json` and its `repeats-table.txt` aggregate (groups arm-scenario-model), Kalari 0.2.3, read through the evidence API rather than as committed rows.

Cost multiplies the `/v1/usage` delta per session by list price, with cached input at full price. Both arms use the same rule. The usage deltas matched OpenCode's own step tokens on the measured probe in <RepoLink path="dev-diary/research/coding-agent.md">research/coding-agent.md</RepoLink>. Totals from the rows: 102 sessions cost about $5.62 at list price (Kalari arm $1.53 over 51 sessions, alone $4.09 over 51).

PASS means the hidden grader passed the final file. RFT means the first consumer run against v2 already passed; it is about the session path, not the final file.

## What is measured

The <DocLink to="judges/">Judges walkthrough</DocLink> reports, per arm over the 45 headline sessions: pass rate (45/45 with Kalari, 34/45 alone), mean cost per session ($0.024 against $0.067), and mean wall time per session (58 s against 166 s). Every figure traces to the repeat rows in the published bundle behind `slides/data/results.json`, read through the evidence API.

Lessons were written, recalled, and enforced across the upgrades. Payouts wrote one lesson in every repeat (15/15 across the three models): the page boundary repeats a record, a token sits on the final page, 429 means retry the identical request, and the last value by ID preserves truth. Settlements recalled that lesson in 15/15 sessions, disbursements in 14/15, and the service attaches every required recalled check before the run, so the new output must satisfy the old lesson. One MiMo disbursements session recalled nothing and still passed; no Kalari session ran in a project whose store lacked the payouts lesson.

## What is not claimed

The frozen 60-run DeepSeek comparison stays as the earlier baseline: $0.019 against $0.082 per session (<RepoLink path="bench/RESULTS.md">bench/RESULTS.md</RepoLink> section 2). Read its zero-lesson count as history about those rows — production validation refused the observer configuration then, so no lesson existed to recall — never as a limit now.

The per-step cost drop ($0.026 to $0.012 to $0.009) is one filmed GLM chain in one shared project, not a repeat result. Payouts and settlements passed alone for every model (15/15 each), so the measured pass-rate edge sits in disbursements, where alone failed 11 of 15 (GLM 5 of 5, DeepSeek 4 of 5, MiMo 2 of 5). Nothing here says DeepSeek verifies on its own: alone it failed disbursements 4 of 5. Five repeats per cell is a small sample. Do not generalize beyond these APIs and models. Alone includes the two sessions stopped at the 900 s cap.
